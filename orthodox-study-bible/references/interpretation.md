## 1. Principles of Orthodox interpretation

- **Scripture lives inside Tradition.** The Church wrote, gathered and recognized the Scriptures, and the Church reads them. Scripture is the highest witness within Holy Tradition, not something opposed to it.
- **Christ is the key.** "Search the Scriptures... they testify of Me" (John 5:39). The Old Testament is read as preparation for, and a hidden presence of, Christ (Luke 24:27, 44).
- **Levels of meaning.** The literal/historical sense, and the spiritual sense (typological, moral, and anagogical — pointing to the age to come). The Antiochene school (St. John Chrysostom) stresses the historical sense; the Alexandrian school (St. Cyril, Origen in part) explores the spiritual sense more freely. Both are used.
- **Consensus of the Fathers.** The safest reading is the one the Fathers share. Individual Fathers sometimes differ; say so honestly.
- **Theosis.** The goal of reading is union with God, so interpretation leads to prayer, repentance and change of life, not only information.
- **Ancestral sin.** Orthodox speak of Adam's sin bringing death and corruption to humanity, not inherited guilt. Keep this in mind for Genesis 3 and Romans 5.

## 2. Key types and their fulfillment

| Old Testament | Fulfillment in the Church's reading |
|---|---|
| Tree of life | The Cross |
| Ark of Noah | The Church; the Flood as baptism (1 Pet 3:20-21) |
| Melchizedek's bread and wine | The Eucharist; Christ the eternal High Priest (Heb 7) |
| Hospitality of Abraham (Gen 18) | Revelation of the Holy Trinity (the Rublev icon) |
| Sacrifice of Isaac (Gen 22) | The Father's offering of the Son; the Resurrection |
| Jacob's ladder | The Theotokos, through whom God came down |
| Burning bush | The Theotokos: carrying God without being consumed |
| Red Sea crossing | Baptism; the Cross dividing the waters |
| Manna | The Eucharist (John 6) |
| Bronze serpent | The Cross (John 3:14) |
| Aaron's rod that budded | The virgin birth |
| Gideon's fleece | The Incarnation in the Virgin's womb |
| Jonah in the fish | Christ's three days in the tomb (Matt 12:40) |
| The three youths in the furnace | The Theotokos; the Son of God in the fire |
| Ezekiel's closed gate (Ezek 44:2) | The ever-virginity of the Theotokos |
| Isaiah's live coal (Isa 6) | The Eucharist; the Theotokos as the tongs |

## 3. Scripture in the services

- **Psalter:** read in full every week in monastic practice, divided into 20 kathismata. Psalms 103 (104) opens Vespers; the Six Psalms (3, 37, 62, 87, 102, 142 LXX) open Matins; Psalm 50 is read at Matins and many other services.
- **Biblical odes:** the nine odes of Matins and the canon are based on biblical songs: Exodus 15, Deuteronomy 32, 1 Kingdoms 2 (Hannah), Habakkuk 3, Isaiah 26, Jonah 2, the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Youths (Daniel 3), and the Magnificat with the Song of Zechariah (Luke 1).
- **Divine Liturgy:** Epistle and Gospel each day; Prokeimenon and Alleluia verses from the Psalms.
- **Vespers of great feasts:** three (sometimes more) Old Testament readings (paroemia) chosen typologically. For example, Theotokos feasts use Genesis 28 (Jacob's ladder), Ezekiel 43-44 (the closed gate) and Proverbs 9 (Wisdom built her house).
- **Holy Saturday Vespers:** fifteen Old Testament readings, including Genesis 1, Jonah, and Daniel 3.
- **Pascha:** the Gospel is John 1:1-17, read in many languages.

## 4. Church year and lectionary

- The church year begins on 1 September.
- The Gospel cycle starts at Pascha with John, then Matthew (after Pentecost), Luke (after the Elevation of the Cross in September), and Mark during the Great Lent weekends.
- The Epistle cycle starts at Pascha with Acts, then Romans and the other epistles.
- **Great Lent:** weekday Old Testament readings from Genesis, Isaiah and Proverbs; Liturgy is mostly not served on weekdays.
- **Twelve Great Feasts:** Nativity of the Theotokos (8 Sep), Elevation of the Cross (14 Sep), Entry of the Theotokos (21 Nov), Nativity of Christ (25 Dec), Theophany (6 Jan), Meeting of the Lord (2 Feb), Annunciation (25 Mar), Palm Sunday, Ascension, Pentecost, Transfiguration (6 Aug), Dormition (15 Aug). Pascha stands above them all.

For an exact reading on a specific date, say that the user should check their jurisdiction's calendar (Old vs. New Calendar) since dates and readings differ.

## 5. Fathers to cite

- **St. John Chrysostom:** homilies on Genesis, Matthew, John, Acts, all of Paul's epistles. The best go-to for New Testament explanations.
- **St. Basil the Great:** Hexaemeron (Genesis 1), homilies on the Psalms.
- **St. Athanasius:** On the Incarnation; Letter to Marcellinus on the Psalms.
- **St. Gregory of Nyssa:** Life of Moses, Homilies on the Song of Songs.
- **St. Cyril of Alexandria:** commentaries on John and Luke, Glaphyra on the Pentateuch.
- **St. Ephrem the Syrian:** hymns, commentary on Genesis and the Diatessaron.
- **St. Irenaeus:** Against Heresies, recapitulation in Christ (Adam/Christ, Eve/Mary).
- **St. Maximus the Confessor:** Ad Thalassium, spiritual interpretation.
- **Blessed Theophylact of Ohrid:** short, clear commentaries on the Gospels and Epistles drawing on Chrysostom.
- **St. Augustine:** respected but read with care in Orthodoxy on some points (e.g. original sin).

Attribute only views you're sure about. A general phrase ("the Fathers commonly read this as...") is better than a wrong attribution.
