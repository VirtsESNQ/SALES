# Epistles and Revelation

The Apostle (the book of Epistles and Acts) is read at the Divine Liturgy before the Gospel, after the Prokeimenon. St. Paul and St. Peter share a feast on 29 June; St. John Chrysostom's homilies cover every one of Paul's letters and are the standard Orthodox commentary.

## Pauline Epistles

**Romans** — Paul's fullest teaching: all have sinned (1-3), justification by faith in Christ (3-5), life in Christ and in the Spirit (6-8), Israel in God's plan (9-11), and Christian life (12-16).
Key passages: "Through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and thus death spread to all men, because all sinned" (5:12; the Greek *eph' hō* is read by the Greek Fathers as "because of which [death] all sinned" — the root of the Orthodox teaching on ancestral sin). Baptism as dying and rising with Christ (6:3-11; read at every baptism). "There is now no condemnation" (8:1). The Spirit helps us pray (8:26). "All things work together for good to those who love God" (8:28). "Present your bodies a living sacrifice" (12:1).
Orthodox emphasis: faith and works are not opposed; salvation is participation in Christ's life by grace (synergy, theosis).

**1 Corinthians** — Problems in a divided, gifted church: factions (1-4), the wisdom of the Cross ("we preach Christ crucified," 1:23), sexual morality and marriage (5-7), food offered to idols (8-10), worship and the Lord's Supper (11), spiritual gifts and the Body of Christ (12-14), the hymn to love (13), and the resurrection (15).
Key passages: "Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us" (5:7). Unworthy communion "not discerning the Lord's body" (11:23-32; read on Holy Thursday). Love is patient and kind (13). "O Death, where is your sting?" (15:55).

**2 Corinthians** — Paul's most personal letter: ministry in weakness, "we have this treasure in earthen vessels" (4:7), "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" (5:17), the ministry of reconciliation, giving generously (8-9), "My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness" (12:9).

**Galatians** — Against requiring circumcision and the Law of Moses for Gentiles. "I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me" (2:20). "As many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ" (3:27; sung at baptisms and on great feasts instead of the Trisagion). "When the fullness of time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman" (4:4; Nativity). Fruit of the Spirit (5:22-23).

**Ephesians** — The Church as the Body and Bride of Christ; God's plan "to gather together in one all things in Christ" (1:10). "By grace you have been saved through faith... not of works" (2:8-10). "One Lord, one faith, one baptism" (4:5). Marriage as an image of Christ and the Church (5:20-33; read at Orthodox weddings). The armor of God (6:10-18).

**Philippians** — A letter of joy from prison. The great hymn: Christ "emptied Himself (*kenosis*), taking the form of a servant... and became obedient to death, even death on a cross" (2:5-11). "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you" (2:12-13; synergy). "I can do all things through Christ" (4:13).

**Colossians** — Christ above all powers: "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation... in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily" (1:15-20; 2:9). Used by St. John of Damascus in defense of icons: the invisible God has made Himself visible. "Set your mind on things above" (3:1-4).

**1-2 Thessalonians** — Paul's earliest letters. Encouragement on the Second Coming and those who have died: "we do not grieve as others who have no hope" (1 Thess 4:13-18; read at funerals). "Pray without ceasing" (1 Thess 5:17; the foundation of the Jesus Prayer). The man of lawlessness and the restrainer (2 Thess 2). "Hold the traditions which you were taught, whether by word or our epistle" (2 Thess 2:15; Scripture and oral Tradition together).

**1-2 Timothy, Titus (Pastoral Epistles)** — Guidance for church leaders: qualifications for bishops (*episkopos*), presbyters and deacons (1 Tim 3; Titus 1), sound doctrine, prayer "for kings and all in authority" (1 Tim 2:1-4; echoed in the Litanies). "Great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifested in the flesh" (1 Tim 3:16). "The Church... the pillar and ground of the truth" (1 Tim 3:15). "All Scripture is given by inspiration of God" (2 Tim 3:16). "I have fought the good fight" (2 Tim 4:7).

**Philemon** — A short letter asking Philemon to receive back his runaway slave Onesimus "as a beloved brother." Shows the Gospel changing human relationships. Tradition says Onesimus later became a bishop.

**Hebrews** — Christ as the eternal High Priest "according to the order of Melchizedek," greater than angels, Moses and the Levitical priesthood. His once-for-all sacrifice fulfills the Temple worship.
Key passages: "The brightness of His glory and the express image of His person" (1:3). "We do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses" (4:15). The new covenant (8). Christ entering the heavenly Holy of Holies with His own blood (9:11-14). The heroes of faith (11; read on the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers and the Sunday of All Saints). "So great a cloud of witnesses" (12:1). "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever" (13:8).
The Orthodox see the Divine Liturgy as participation in this heavenly worship.

## Catholic (General) Epistles

**James** — Practical faith: "Faith without works is dead" (2:26). Taming the tongue (3). "Draw near to God and He will draw near to you" (4:8). "Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil" (5:14-15; the Mystery of Holy Unction, read at the service). "Confess your sins to one another" (5:16).

**1 Peter** — To suffering Christians: "a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation" (2:9). Christ "went and preached to the spirits in prison" (3:19) and the ark as a figure of baptism (3:20-21) — key texts for Christ's descent into Hades (the Paschal icon). "Your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion" (5:8; read at Compline).

**2 Peter** — "That you may become partakers of the divine nature" (1:4) — the classic proof-text for theosis. The Transfiguration witnessed (1:16-18). "No prophecy of Scripture is of any private interpretation" (1:20-21). The Day of the Lord and new heavens and new earth (3).

**1 John** — "God is light" (1:5); "God is love" (4:8, 16). "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us" (1:9). Love of God shown in love for the brethren. "The Word of life" seen and touched (1:1-3) against those who deny Christ came in the flesh.

**2-3 John** — Short letters on walking in truth and love, and on hospitality.

**Jude** — Contend "for the faith which was once for all delivered to the saints" (3). Quotes 1 Enoch and the archangel Michael disputing with the devil (9).

## Revelation (the Apocalypse)
**Overview:** St. John's vision on Patmos: letters to seven churches (2-3), the heavenly worship around the throne and the Lamb (4-5), the seals, trumpets and bowls of judgment (6-16), the fall of Babylon (17-18), the marriage supper of the Lamb and the final victory (19-20), and the new heaven and new earth, the New Jerusalem (21-22).
**Orthodox reading:**
- It is the only New Testament book **not read in the Divine Liturgy**, because its place in the canon was accepted later in the East.
- It is read as an image of heavenly worship (4-5: elders, incense, "Holy, holy, holy," "Worthy is the Lamb"), which shapes the Liturgy. The woman clothed with the sun (12) is read as the Church and also as the Theotokos.
- The Orthodox Fathers did not read the "thousand years" (20) as a literal future kingdom on earth (chiliasm was rejected); it is the present age of the Church.
- Christ: "I am the Alpha and the Omega" (1:8; 22:13). "Behold, I make all things new" (21:5). "Behold, I stand at the door and knock" (3:20). "Even so, come, Lord Jesus!" (22:20).
**Fathers:** St. Andrew of Caesarea's commentary (6th century) is the standard Orthodox commentary; St. Arethas.
