# Gospels and Acts

The Gospel Book sits on the altar of every Orthodox church as Christ Himself. It is carried in procession at the Little Entrance, kissed by the faithful, and read by the deacon or priest while everyone stands. At Pascha, John 1 is read; the lectionary then moves through John, Matthew, Luke and Mark over the year.

The four Evangelists and their symbols (from Ezekiel 1 and Revelation 4): Matthew — man (Christ's human genealogy); Mark — lion (the voice crying in the wilderness); Luke — ox (the sacrifice, beginning at the Temple); John — eagle (soaring to the divinity of the Word).

## Matthew
**Written for** Jewish Christians, showing Jesus as the Messiah who fulfills the Law and the Prophets ("that it might be fulfilled...").
**Structure:** Infancy (1-2); five great discourses — the Sermon on the Mount (5-7), mission (10), parables (13), the Church (18), the end times (24-25) — and the Passion and Resurrection (26-28).
**Key passages:** Genealogy from Abraham (1; read on the Sunday before Nativity). The Magi (2). The Beatitudes (5:3-12; sung at the Divine Liturgy as the Third Antiphon). The Lord's Prayer (6:9-13). "Seek first the kingdom of God" (6:33). Peter's confession and "on this rock I will build My Church" (16:13-19; Orthodox Fathers mostly read the rock as Peter's confession of faith, shared by all bishops who hold it). The Transfiguration (17:1-9; feast 6 August). Parable of the Talents (25:14-30) and the Last Judgment, "inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these" (25:31-46; read on Meatfare Sunday). The institution of the Eucharist (26:26-29). The Great Commission (28:18-20; read at baptisms).
**Fathers:** St. John Chrysostom's 90 Homilies on Matthew; Blessed Theophylact.

## Mark
**The shortest Gospel,** traditionally from St. Peter's preaching in Rome. Fast-moving ("immediately"), it focuses on Christ's deeds and on the Cross; the disciples are slow to understand.
**Key passages:** "The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God" (1:1; the Baptism of Christ). The paralytic let down through the roof (2:1-12; 2nd Sunday of Lent). "Lord, I believe; help my unbelief!" (9:24; 4th Sunday of Lent, with St. John Climacus). "Whoever desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross" (8:34; Sunday of the Cross, 3rd of Lent). James and John ask for places of honor; "the Son of Man came to serve" (10:32-45; 5th Sunday of Lent). The myrrhbearing women at the tomb (16:1-8; Sunday of the Myrrhbearers). The longer ending (16:9-20) is part of the Orthodox text and is read at the eleven Resurrection Gospels of Sunday Matins.

## Luke
**Written by** St. Luke the physician, companion of St. Paul, also the author of Acts. Tradition says he painted the first icon of the Theotokos. Emphasizes prayer, the Holy Spirit, mercy to the poor, sinners, women and outsiders, and joy.
**Key passages:**
- 1:26-38 — The Annunciation: "Rejoice, highly favored one, the Lord is with you" (the basis of "Theotokos Virgin, rejoice"). "Behold the handmaid of the Lord."
- 1:39-56 — The Visitation and the Magnificat ("My soul magnifies the Lord"), sung at Matins as the Ninth Ode with "More honorable than the cherubim." Read at nearly every feast of the Theotokos, with 10:38-42 and 11:27-28.
- 1:68-79 — The song of Zechariah (Benedictus).
- 2:1-20 — The Nativity, the shepherds and the angels' "Glory to God in the highest."
- 2:22-40 — The Meeting of the Lord in the Temple; St. Simeon's prayer "Now let Your servant depart in peace" (sung at every Vespers).
- 4:16-21 — Christ reads Isaiah 61 in Nazareth.
- 10:25-37 — The Good Samaritan. The Fathers read the man as Adam, the Samaritan as Christ, the inn as the Church, the oil and wine as the Mysteries.
- 15:11-32 — The Prodigal Son (Sunday before Meatfare). "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you."
- 16:19-31 — The rich man and Lazarus.
- 18:9-14 — The Publican and the Pharisee (start of the Triodion). The publican's prayer, "God, be merciful to me a sinner," is the root of the Jesus Prayer.
- 19:1-10 — Zacchaeus (the Sunday that announces Lent is coming).
- 22:39-46 — Gethsemane: sweat like drops of blood.
- 23:39-43 — The wise thief: "Remember me when You come into Your kingdom" (sung before Communion and at the Typika).
- 24:13-35 — The road to Emmaus: Christ known "in the breaking of bread."

## John
**The theological Gospel**, written last by the beloved disciple, St. John the Theologian. Seven signs, long discourses, "I AM" sayings, and a focus on Christ's divinity, light, life, truth and love.
**Key passages:**
- 1:1-18 — "In the beginning was the Word... and the Word became flesh." Read at Paschal Liturgy, often in many languages.
- 1:29 — "Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world."
- 2:1-11 — The wedding at Cana; the Theotokos: "Whatever He says to you, do it." Read at weddings.
- 3:1-21 — Nicodemus: "born of water and the Spirit" (baptism); "God so loved the world" (3:16).
- 4:5-42 — The Samaritan woman (St. Photini; 5th Sunday of Pascha): "God is Spirit."
- 5:1-15 — The paralytic at the pool of Bethesda (4th Sunday of Pascha).
- 6 — The bread of life: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you have no life in you" (6:53). The basis of Orthodox Eucharistic faith.
- 9 — The man born blind (6th Sunday of Pascha): Christ makes clay as at the creation of Adam.
- 10 — The Good Shepherd.
- 11 — The raising of Lazarus; "I am the resurrection and the life." Lazarus Saturday before Palm Sunday.
- 13-17 — The Last Supper discourse: washing of feet, "I am the way, the truth and the life" (14:6), the Comforter (14-16), the vine and the branches (15), the High-Priestly prayer "that they all may be one" (17).
- 15:26 — "The Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father." The basis for the Creed's words on the Holy Spirit (without the Western *filioque*).
- 19:25-27 — "Woman, behold your son": the Theotokos entrusted to John and to all the faithful.
- 19:34 — Blood and water from Christ's side: the Mysteries of the Eucharist and baptism. Recalled when water and wine are mixed at the Proskomedia.
- 20:19-31 — Christ breathes the Holy Spirit on the apostles; doubting Thomas: "My Lord and my God!" (Thomas Sunday).
- 21:15-17 — Peter restored by the threefold "Do you love Me?"
**Fathers:** St. John Chrysostom (88 Homilies on John), St. Cyril of Alexandria (Commentary on John), St. Augustine (Tractates on John).

## Acts of the Apostles
**Overview:** By St. Luke. From the Ascension and Pentecost through the growth of the Church in Jerusalem (1-7), Samaria and the first Gentiles (8-12), to St. Paul's missions and his journey to Rome (13-28).
**Key passages:** The Ascension (1:1-12). Pentecost: tongues of fire and Peter's sermon (2; read at Pentecost). "They continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread and in prayers" (2:42) — a portrait of the Church. The seven deacons and St. Stephen the first martyr (6-7; feast 27 December). Philip and the Ethiopian eunuch reading Isaiah 53 (8:26-40). Saul's conversion on the road to Damascus (9). Peter and Cornelius: the Gospel for the Gentiles (10). The Council of Jerusalem (15) — the pattern for the Ecumenical Councils. Paul at the Areopagus (17; St. Dionysius the Areopagite converted).
**In the services:** Read at the Divine Liturgy daily from Pascha to Pentecost. In many monasteries Acts is read through in the church on Pascha night.
**Fathers:** St. John Chrysostom (55 Homilies on Acts).
