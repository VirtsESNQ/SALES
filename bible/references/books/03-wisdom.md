# Poetic and Wisdom books

## Psalms (151 psalms, LXX numbering)
**The Church's prayer book.** The Psalter is prayed in every service. It is divided into 20 *kathismata* (sections) and read through every week in monasteries, twice a week during Great Lent. See `canon-and-numbering.md` for LXX vs. Hebrew numbers.

**Psalms the Orthodox know best (LXX numbering, Hebrew in brackets)**
- 1 — "Blessed is the man." Sung at Saturday-evening Vespers.
- 2 — "You are My Son, today I have begotten You." The eternal begetting of the Son; quoted in Acts 13:33 and Hebrews.
- 3, 37 (38), 62 (63), 87 (88), 102 (103), 142 (143) — the **Six Psalms** that open Matins. Read in silence and dim light, without movement; the faithful stand as before the Last Judgment.
- 8 — "Out of the mouth of babes" (Palm Sunday).
- 21 (22) — "My God, My God, why have You forsaken me?" Christ prays it on the Cross; it foretells the piercing of hands and feet and the dividing of His garments. Read at the Royal Hours of Holy Friday.
- 22 (23) — "The Lord is my shepherd." Prayed before Holy Communion.
- 23 (24) — "Lift up your gates" — Christ's ascension and His descent into Hades; used at the consecration of a church.
- 33 (34) — "Taste and see that the Lord is good." Communion hymn.
- 44 (45) — "The queen stood at Your right hand." Applied to the Theotokos; source of the prokeimenon of her feasts.
- 50 (51) — "Have mercy on me, O God." Psalm of repentance, said at Matins, before Communion, at the Hours and in many prayers.
- 67 (68) — "Let God arise and let His enemies be scattered." The verses of the Paschal stichera.
- 90 (91) — "He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High." Protection; at Compline and the Sixth Hour.
- 102 (103) and 145 (146) — the Typical Psalms at the start of the Divine Liturgy.
- 103 (104) — The Psalm of creation, which opens Vespers.
- 109 (110) — "The Lord said to my Lord, sit at My right hand... a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek."
- 117 (118) — "The stone which the builders rejected." "God is the Lord and has appeared to us" at Matins.
- 118 (119) — the long psalm on the Law; sung at funerals and at Saturday Matins, and with "the Lamentations" on Holy Saturday Matins.
- 133 (134) and 140 (141) — "Let my prayer arise as incense," the evening psalm of Vespers and the Presanctified Liturgy.
- 136 (137) — "By the waters of Babylon," sung on the three Sundays before Great Lent.
- 148-150 — the Praises at Matins.
- 151 — David's short psalm about being chosen though the youngest and defeating Goliath. In the LXX, not in the Hebrew Bible, and not in the bundled text.

**Fathers:** St. Athanasius' Letter to Marcellinus (the Psalms as a mirror of the soul, with a psalm for every situation), St. Basil's Homilies on the Psalms, St. John Chrysostom's commentary, St. Augustine's Expositions.

## Job
**Overview:** A righteous man loses his wealth, children and health. His friends insist he must have sinned; Job protests his innocence and asks God for an answer. God speaks out of the whirlwind about the wonders of creation (38-41). Job repents of speaking about what he did not understand and is restored.
**Key passages:** "The Lord gave and the Lord has taken away; blessed be the name of the Lord" (1:21). "I know that my Redeemer lives... in my flesh I shall see God" (19:25-26; the resurrection). God's speeches from the whirlwind (38-41).
**In the Church:** Job is read at Vespers during Holy Week (Monday to Wednesday) as a type of the suffering Christ. St. Job is feasted on 6 May. St. Gregory the Great's *Moralia on Job* is a major commentary.

## Proverbs
**Overview:** Collections of wise sayings, mostly attributed to Solomon. The opening chapters personify Wisdom as a woman calling people to life.
**Key passages:** "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" (1:7; 9:10). Wisdom present at creation (8:22-31): the Fathers read Wisdom as the Son and Word of God. The controversy over 8:22 ("The Lord created me" in the LXX) was central in the Arian debates; St. Athanasius applied it to the Son's human nature. "Wisdom has built her house" (9:1-11): read at Theotokos feasts — the Theotokos is the house in which Wisdom, Christ, lived. The virtuous woman (31:10-31).
**In the services:** Read at Vespers every weekday of Great Lent.

## Ecclesiastes
**Overview:** "Vanity of vanities, all is vanity" (1:2). The Preacher looks at a life lived "under the sun" and finds it empty without God. Conclusion: "Fear God and keep His commandments" (12:13).
**Fathers:** St. Gregory of Nyssa (Homilies on Ecclesiastes) and Evagrius read it as teaching detachment from passing things, the first step in the spiritual life. "A time to be born and a time to die" (3:1-8).

## Song of Songs
**Overview:** Love poetry between a bridegroom and bride.
**Orthodox reading:** Always read spiritually: Christ and the Church, God and the soul, and the Theotokos. St. Gregory of Nyssa's Homilies on the Song (the soul's endless ascent into God), Origen, St. Nilus. Phrases like "a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed" (4:12) are applied to the ever-virgin Theotokos.
**In the services:** The image of the Bridegroom underlies the Bridegroom Matins of Holy Week.

## Wisdom of Solomon
**Overview:** Written in Greek shortly before Christ. Teaches the immortality of the righteous, praises Wisdom, and reflects on the Exodus.
**Key passages:** "The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God" (3:1-9; read at feasts of martyrs and at funerals). The persecution of the righteous man who calls himself son of God (2:12-20; read as a prophecy of Christ's Passion). Wisdom as "the brightness of the everlasting light... the image of His goodness" (7:25-26; echoed in Heb 1:3 about the Son). "God did not make death" (1:13); "God created man to be immortal... through the devil's envy death entered the world" (2:23-24) — central to the Orthodox teaching on the fall.
**In the services:** Read at Vespers of feasts of saints and of Pentecost.

## Wisdom of Sirach (Ecclesiasticus)
**Overview:** Practical and religious teaching by Jesus son of Sirach (c. 180 BC), translated into Greek by his grandson. Covers family, friendship, speech, wealth, worship, and ends with "the praise of famous men" (44-50).
**Key passages:** "My son, if you come to serve the Lord, prepare your soul for temptation" (2:1). Wisdom living in Israel (24). True friendship (6:5-17). "In all you do, remember your end, and you will never sin" (7:36). The fathers of Israel (44-50).
**In the Church:** Much quoted by the Fathers and in monastic teaching (St. John Climacus, the Desert Fathers).
