# The Prophets

The Orthodox Church honors the prophets as saints and reads them as the clearest Old Testament witnesses to Christ. In the Septuagint order the Twelve "minor" prophets come first, then Isaiah, Jeremiah (with Baruch, Lamentations and the Epistle of Jeremiah), Ezekiel and Daniel.

## The Twelve

**Hosea** — Told to marry an unfaithful wife, Gomer, as a living image of God's faithful love for unfaithful Israel. "Out of Egypt I called My son" (11:1; Matt 2:15). "I desire mercy and not sacrifice" (6:6). "O Death, where is your sting?" (13:14; quoted by St. Paul and in St. John Chrysostom's Paschal Homily). Read at Holy Saturday and the Sunday of the Myrrhbearers.

**Amos** — A shepherd from Tekoa denounces injustice and empty worship in the northern kingdom. "Let justice roll down like waters" (5:24). Promises to restore "the tabernacle of David that has fallen" (9:11; Acts 15:16).

**Micah** — "But you, Bethlehem... out of you shall come forth the One to be Ruler in Israel, whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting" (5:2; read at Nativity Vespers). "What does the Lord require of you but to do justly, to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God" (6:8). "My people, what have I done to you?" (6:3) is echoed in the Holy Friday hymns.

**Joel** — A locust plague as a call to repentance: "Rend your heart and not your garments" (2:13; read at the start of Lent). "I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh" (2:28-32 English = 3:1-5 LXX/Hebrew; fulfilled at Pentecost, Acts 2:16-21; read at Pentecost Vespers).

**Obadiah** — The shortest book: judgment on Edom for gloating over Jerusalem's fall; "the kingdom shall be the Lord's."

**Jonah** — Flees God's call, is swallowed by a great fish for three days, prays (the Prayer of Jonah, 2:2-9 — the Sixth Ode of the canon), preaches in Nineveh, which repents. Christ calls His three days in the tomb "the sign of the prophet Jonah" (Matt 12:39-41). The whole book is read at Vespers of Holy Saturday. Lesson: God's mercy extends to the nations.

**Nahum** — The fall of Nineveh. "Behold, on the mountains the feet of him who brings good tidings" (1:15).

**Habakkuk** — The prophet asks why God allows evil and waits for His answer: "The just shall live by his faith" (2:4; Rom 1:17). His prayer (3) is the Fourth Ode of the canon; "God will come from Teman, and the Holy One from the shady mountain" (3:3 LXX) is read as the Incarnation from the Virgin. The Fourth Ode of the Paschal canon: "On divine watch let God-inspired Habakkuk stand with us."

**Zephaniah** — The Day of the Lord, then joy: "Sing, O daughter of Zion... the Lord is in your midst" (3:14-17); read at the Annunciation and Palm Sunday.

**Haggai** — Calls the returned exiles to rebuild the Temple: "The glory of this latter house shall be greater than the former" (2:9), because Christ would enter it.

**Zechariah** — Visions of restoration. "Behold, your King comes to you... lowly and riding on a donkey" (9:9; Palm Sunday). "Thirty pieces of silver" (11:12-13). "They shall look on Me whom they pierced" (12:10; John 19:37). "Strike the Shepherd and the sheep will be scattered" (13:7). Living waters from Jerusalem (14:8).

**Malachi** — "From the rising of the sun to its setting My name shall be great among the nations, and in every place incense... and a pure offering" (1:11). The Fathers saw the Eucharist offered everywhere. "Behold, I send My messenger" (3:1; St. John the Baptist). "The Sun of righteousness shall arise with healing in His wings" (4:2 English = 3:20; Christ, "the Sun of righteousness" in the Nativity troparion). "I will send you Elijah the prophet" (4:5).

## Isaiah
**"The fifth Gospel."** Isaiah (8th century BC) speaks to Judah of judgment and hope, then (40-66) of comfort, return, the Servant of the Lord and new creation.

**Key passages**
- 1:16-20 — "Wash yourselves, make yourselves clean... though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow." Read at Theophany Vespers and the start of Lent.
- 6 — The vision of the Lord on His throne and the seraphim crying "Holy, holy, holy" — the origin of the *Sanctus* in the Divine Liturgy. The live coal touching Isaiah's lips is the Eucharist touching the lips of communicants ("Behold, this has touched your lips," said by the priest after Communion); the tongs that hold it are the Theotokos.
- 7:14 — "Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a Son, and shall call His name Immanuel" — the LXX has *parthenos*, virgin (Matt 1:23).
- 9:6-7 — "For unto us a Child is born... Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God... Angel of Great Counsel" (LXX). Sung at the Nativity and in Great Compline: "God is with us."
- 11:1-10 — The shoot from the stem of Jesse, the seven gifts of the Spirit, the wolf lying with the lamb. The Theotokos is "the rod from the root of Jesse."
- 35 — The desert blooms, the blind see and the lame leap.
- 40:1-11 — "Comfort, comfort My people... the voice of one crying in the wilderness" (St. John the Baptist).
- 42:1-9; 49:1-6; 50:4-9; 52:13-53:12 — The Servant Songs. Chapter 53, "He was wounded for our transgressions... led as a sheep to the slaughter," is the clearest prophecy of the Passion; read at the Royal Hours of Holy Friday and recalled at the Proskomedia (preparation of the gifts), where the priest says "As a sheep He was led to the slaughter" while cutting the Lamb.
- 60 — "Arise, shine, for your light has come" — the Paschal canon: "Shine, shine, O new Jerusalem."
- 61:1-2 — "The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me..." read by Christ in Nazareth (Luke 4:18).
- 66 — New heavens and a new earth.

**Fathers:** St. Jerome ("ignorance of Scripture is ignorance of Christ" — in his Isaiah commentary), St. Cyril of Alexandria, St. Basil (on 1-16), St. John Chrysostom (on 1-8).

## Jeremiah
**Overview:** A prophet during Judah's last years and the fall of Jerusalem (587 BC); persecuted, imprisoned, weeping over his people. LXX chapter order differs from English Bibles in the middle (oracles against the nations).
**Key passages:** His call: "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you" (1:5). The potter and the clay (18). "Like a gentle lamb led to the slaughter" (11:19; type of Christ). The new covenant written on the heart (31:31-34 English = 38:31-34 LXX; quoted in Heb 8; read at Holy Thursday Vespers). Rachel weeping for her children (31:15; the Holy Innocents, Matt 2:18).

## Baruch
**Overview:** Attributed to Jeremiah's scribe. A confession of sins, a poem on Wisdom and a song of comfort to Jerusalem.
**Key passage:** "This is our God, and no other can be compared with Him... afterward He appeared on earth and lived among men" (3:36-38). Read by the Fathers as a prophecy of the Incarnation; read at Nativity Vespers.

## Lamentations
**Overview:** Five poems of grief over the destroyed Jerusalem, traditionally by Jeremiah. "The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases... great is Your faithfulness" (3:22-23). "Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by?" (1:12), applied to Christ's sufferings. Shapes the tone of Holy Saturday's Lamentations at the tomb.

## Epistle of Jeremiah
**Overview:** A letter warning the exiles in Babylon against idols, which "cannot save themselves." Printed as Baruch chapter 6 in the bundled KJV text.

## Ezekiel
**Overview:** A priest-prophet in exile in Babylon. Visions of God's glory leaving and returning to the Temple.
**Key passages:** The vision of the throne-chariot with the four living creatures (1; the four Evangelists' symbols — man, lion, ox, eagle). The Shepherd who seeks the lost (34). "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you" (36:25-27). The valley of dry bones (37:1-14; the general resurrection; read at Holy Saturday Matins). The closed eastern gate that "the Lord God of Israel has entered by it; therefore it shall be shut" (44:1-4; the ever-virginity of the Theotokos; read at Theotokos feasts). The river flowing from the Temple (47; baptism and the Spirit).

## Daniel (with the Greek additions)
**Overview:** A young exile in Babylon faithful to God at the royal court. Stories (1-6) and visions (7-12). The Greek adds the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Young Men in chapter 3, Susanna (13) and Bel and the Dragon (14).
**Key passages**
- 2 — Nebuchadnezzar's dream: a stone "cut out without hands" destroys the statue and becomes a mountain. Christ, born of the Virgin without human seed; sung in the Theotokos hymns ("the mountain from which the stone was cut without hands").
- 3 — The three young men (Ananias, Azarias, Misael) in the furnace, unharmed, with a fourth "like the Son of God." The furnace that did not burn them is the Theotokos who held the divine fire. Their hymns ("Blessed are You, O Lord God of our fathers" and "Bless the Lord, all you works of the Lord") are the Seventh and Eighth Odes of the canon. Read at Holy Saturday Vespers. Commemorated on 17 December, on the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers.
- 6 — Daniel in the lions' den.
- 7:9-14 — The Ancient of Days and "one like the Son of Man" coming on the clouds — the title Christ takes for Himself; icons depict the Ancient of Days as Christ.
- 9:24-27 — The seventy weeks.
- 12:2 — "Many of those who sleep in the dust shall awake."
- 13 (Susanna) — A chaste woman falsely accused and saved by young Daniel's wisdom. Read at Vespers of Holy Saturday in some traditions.
- 14 (Bel and the Dragon) — Daniel exposes idols; fed in the lions' den by the prophet Habakkuk.
**Saints:** The Prophet Daniel and the Three Holy Youths, feasted on 17 December.

## Prayer of Manasseh
**Overview:** The repentance prayer of the wicked king Manasseh in captivity (2 Chr 33). "I am not worthy to look up and see the height of heaven because of the multitude of my iniquities... I bend the knee of my heart." Read at Great Compline. In the bundled text as "Prayer of Manasses."
