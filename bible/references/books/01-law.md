# The Law (Pentateuch)

Five books traditionally attributed to Moses. In the Orthodox reading they tell of creation, the fall and God's first steps in saving humanity, and they are full of types of Christ, the Theotokos, baptism and the Eucharist.

## Genesis

**Overview:** Creation (1-2), the fall (3), the spread of sin (4-11: Cain, the Flood, Babel), and the patriarchs Abraham (12-25), Isaac and Jacob (25-36) and Joseph (37-50).

**Key passages**
- 1:1-2:3 — Creation in six days and the seventh day of rest. Read at Vespers of Holy Saturday and of Theophany and Nativity.
- 1:26-27 — Man made "in the image and likeness" of God. The Fathers (St. Basil, St. Gregory of Nyssa, St. John of Damascus) distinguish *image* (reason, freedom — given) from *likeness* (virtue, union with God — to be attained). This is the basis of theosis.
- 2:7 — God breathes life into Adam.
- 3:15 — "I will put enmity between you and the woman..." Called the *Protoevangelium*, the first gospel: the Seed of the woman (Christ, born of the Virgin) crushes the serpent.
- 3:22-24 — Expulsion from Paradise. Remembered on Forgiveness Sunday, the day before Great Lent.
- 6-9 — Noah and the Flood. The ark = the Church; the Flood = baptism (1 Pet 3:20-21).
- 14:18-20 — Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest, brings bread and wine. Type of Christ's eternal priesthood and the Eucharist (Heb 7).
- 18 — Three visitors at the oak of Mamre (the Hospitality of Abraham). The Church sees a revelation of the Holy Trinity; this is the subject of St. Andrei Rublev's icon of the Trinity.
- 22 — The binding of Isaac (the Akedah). Isaac carries the wood up the mountain as Christ carries the Cross; the Father "does not spare His only Son" (Rom 8:32). Read at Holy Saturday Vespers.
- 28:10-17 — Jacob's ladder, joining heaven and earth. Read at feasts of the Theotokos: she is the ladder by which God came down.
- 32:22-32 — Jacob wrestles with God and is named Israel.
- 37-50 — Joseph: betrayed by his brothers, sold for silver, "dead" and raised to glory, then forgives and saves them. One of the clearest types of Christ. Joseph is commemorated in the Bridegroom Matins of Holy Monday.
- 49:10 — "The scepter shall not depart from Judah..." Read as a prophecy of Christ.

**Fathers:** St. John Chrysostom (67 Homilies on Genesis), St. Basil (Hexaemeron on the days of creation), St. Ephrem (Commentary on Genesis), St. Ambrose (On Paradise).

**Orthodox emphases:** Ancestral sin (death and corruption passed on, not inherited guilt); humanity as priest of creation; Paradise as communion with God.

**In the services:** Read on weekdays of Great Lent at Vespers, straight through from chapter 1.

## Exodus

**Overview:** Israel enslaved in Egypt (1), the call of Moses (2-4), the plagues and the Passover (5-12), the crossing of the Red Sea (13-15), the journey to Sinai with manna and water from the rock (16-18), the covenant and the Law (19-24), the tabernacle (25-31; 35-40), and the golden calf (32-34).

**Key passages**
- 3:1-14 — The burning bush. The bush burned but was not consumed: the Theotokos who carried God in her womb without being consumed. God reveals His name, "I AM" (in the LXX *ho ōn*, "He Who Is" — the letters written in Christ's halo in icons).
- 12 — The Passover lamb, without blemish, whose blood saves from death. Christ is "our Passover" (1 Cor 5:7). Pascha takes its name from this.
- 14-15 — Crossing the Red Sea. A type of baptism (1 Cor 10:1-2). Moses' song (15:1-19) is the First Ode of the canon at Matins.
- 15:23-25 — Bitter water made sweet by a tree: the Cross.
- 16 — Manna, bread from heaven: the Eucharist (John 6:31-35).
- 17:1-7 — Water from the rock: "the Rock was Christ" (1 Cor 10:4).
- 17:8-13 — Moses holds up his arms and Israel wins: a figure of the Cross. Read at the Elevation of the Cross.
- 20 — The Ten Commandments.
- 24:15-18; 33:18-23; 34:29-35 — Moses on the mountain and his shining face. Linked to the Transfiguration.
- 25-31, 40 — The tabernacle: the ark, the lampstand, the jar of manna, the veil. These are all used as titles of the Theotokos (the Ark, the Lampstand, the Golden Jar). Exodus 40 is read at Vespers on Theotokos feasts.

**Fathers:** St. Gregory of Nyssa (The Life of Moses, on the soul's ascent to God), Origen (Homilies on Exodus), St. Cyril of Alexandria (Glaphyra).

## Leviticus

**Overview:** The laws of sacrifice (1-7), the priesthood (8-10), clean and unclean (11-15), the Day of Atonement (16), and the Holiness Code (17-27).

**Key passages**
- 1-7 — The five kinds of offerings. Hebrews teaches that all of them point to the one sacrifice of Christ.
- 12 — Purification after childbirth, and the offering at 40 days. Background for the Meeting of the Lord in the Temple (Luke 2:22-24) and for the Orthodox 40th-day churching of mother and child.
- 16 — The Day of Atonement: the high priest enters the Holy of Holies with blood; the scapegoat. Fulfilled in Christ, who enters the heavenly sanctuary with His own blood (Heb 9).
- 19:2 — "Be holy, for I am holy."
- 19:18 — "Love your neighbor as yourself," quoted by Christ as the second great commandment.

**Orthodox reading:** The ritual laws are fulfilled and changed in Christ, not simply thrown away. Their spiritual meaning (purity of heart, offering oneself to God) remains.

## Numbers

**Overview:** Census and organization of the camp at Sinai (1-10), the journey through the wilderness with complaints and rebellions (11-25), and preparation to enter the land (26-36).

**Key passages**
- 6:22-27 — The priestly blessing: "The Lord bless you and keep you..."
- 17 — Aaron's rod that blossomed, a dry stick bearing fruit: the Virgin who gave birth.
- 20:1-13 — Water from the rock at Meribah.
- 21:4-9 — The bronze serpent lifted up; whoever looks at it lives. Christ applies this to His Crucifixion (John 3:14-15).
- 22-24 — Balaam and his donkey. Balaam's prophecy "A star shall come out of Jacob" (24:17) is read as a prophecy of Christ and connected with the star of the Magi. Read at Vespers of the Nativity.

## Deuteronomy

**Overview:** Moses' farewell sermons on the plains of Moab: he retells the journey, restates the Law and calls Israel to love and obey God. Ends with Moses' song, blessing and death.

**Key passages**
- 6:4-9 — The *Shema*: "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart..." Christ calls it the first commandment.
- 8:3 — "Man shall not live by bread alone." Quoted by Christ in the temptation in the desert.
- 18:15-19 — "The Lord will raise up for you a Prophet like me." Applied to Christ (Acts 3:22).
- 30:15-20 — "I have set before you life and death... choose life."
- 32 — The Song of Moses. The Second Ode of the canon (in practice sung only during Great Lent).
- 34 — Death of Moses on Mount Nebo.

**Fathers:** Christ answers all three temptations in the desert with Deuteronomy (8:3; 6:16; 6:13). The Fathers see this as the new Israel succeeding in the wilderness where the old one failed.
