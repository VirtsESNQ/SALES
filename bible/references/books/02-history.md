# Historical books

These books tell the story of Israel from the entry into the land to the time of the Maccabees. Orthodox Christians read them as history that also prefigures Christ and the Church. Names follow the Septuagint (1-4 Kingdoms, Paraleipomenon); the bundled KJV text uses the names Samuel and Kings.

## Joshua
**Overview:** The crossing of the Jordan and conquest of Canaan (1-12), division of the land (13-22), Joshua's farewell (23-24).
**Key passages:** Rahab and the scarlet cord (2; a type of the Church of the Gentiles saved by the blood of Christ; Heb 11:31). The Jordan stopped for the ark (3-4; a type of baptism and of Theophany, read at Theophany Vespers). The walls of Jericho (6). "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord" (24:15).
**Christ:** "Joshua" in Greek is *Iesous*, Jesus. The Fathers (St. Justin, Origen) read Joshua leading the people into the promised land as Christ leading us into the Kingdom, which Moses (the Law) could not do.

## Judges
**Overview:** Cycles of sin, oppression, crying out and deliverance through judges: Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah, Samson. Ends in chaos: "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (21:25).
**Key passages:** Gideon's fleece wet with dew while the ground is dry, and the reverse (6:36-40): the Incarnation, God coming down on the Virgin like dew. Read at Theotokos feasts and sung in the Nativity hymns. The birth of Samson announced by an angel (13).
**Fathers:** St. Ambrose and St. Augustine treat Samson as a figure of Christ.

## Ruth
**Overview:** A Moabite widow stays loyal to her mother-in-law Naomi ("where you go, I will go," 1:16) and marries Boaz, the kinsman-redeemer. She becomes the great-grandmother of David.
**Christ:** A Gentile brought into Israel and into the line of the Messiah (Matt 1:5). Boaz the redeemer is a type of Christ, Ruth a type of the Church of the nations.

## 1 Kingdoms (1 Samuel)
**Overview:** Birth of Samuel and Hannah's prayer (1-2), the ark taken by the Philistines (4-6), Israel asks for a king; Saul (8-15), David anointed and fights Goliath, Saul's jealousy (16-31).
**Key passages:** Hannah's song "My heart rejoices in the Lord" (2:1-10; the Third Ode of the canon, closely echoed by the Magnificat). "To obey is better than sacrifice" (15:22). David chosen though the youngest: "the Lord looks at the heart" (16:7). David and Goliath (17), read as Christ conquering the devil.
**Christ:** Hannah's barren womb opened is a type of the Theotokos' miraculous birth from the barren Anna and Joachim; the anointed king (*christos*) David is the great type of Christ.

## 2 Kingdoms (2 Samuel)
**Overview:** David's reign: king over Judah, then all Israel; Jerusalem made the capital and the ark brought there (6); God's covenant with David (7); David's sin with Bathsheba and Nathan's rebuke (11-12); Absalom's revolt (13-19).
**Key passages:** David dancing before the ark (6). The covenant: "I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever... I will be his Father, and he shall be My son" (7:12-16), fulfilled in Christ, Son of David. Nathan: "You are the man!" (12:7). The setting of Psalm 50 (51), the great Orthodox psalm of repentance.

## 3 Kingdoms (1 Kings)
**Overview:** Solomon's wisdom and the Temple (1-11), the kingdom split into Israel (north) and Judah (south) (12), Elijah's ministry (17-21).
**Key passages:** Solomon's request for wisdom (3). Dedication of the Temple and the glory filling it (8; read at the consecration of a church). Elijah fed by ravens, the widow of Zarephath, raising her son (17). Elijah and the prophets of Baal on Carmel: "How long will you falter between two opinions?" (18). The "still small voice" at Horeb (19:11-13), central to Orthodox teaching on stillness (hesychia).
**Saints:** The Prophet Elijah (Elias) is feasted on 20 July; he appears at the Transfiguration.

## 4 Kingdoms (2 Kings)
**Overview:** Elijah taken up in a fiery chariot and Elisha's ministry (2-13), the fall of the northern kingdom to Assyria (17), the reforms of Hezekiah and Josiah, and the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon (25).
**Key passages:** Elijah's ascension and his mantle passed to Elisha (2). Naaman healed by washing seven times in the Jordan (5; a type of baptism, read at Theophany). Iron axe head floating (6:1-7; the Cross raising fallen humanity). The finding of the book of the Law under Josiah (22).

## 1-2 Paraleipomenon (1-2 Chronicles)
**Overview:** "Things left out" (*paraleipomena*): a retelling of history from Adam to the Exile with focus on David, the Temple, worship and the kings of Judah. Ends with Cyrus' decree to rebuild (2 Chr 36).
**Key passages:** Genealogies (1 Chr 1-9). David organizes the Temple singers and Levites (1 Chr 15-16, 23-26). Solomon's prayer at the Temple (2 Chr 6-7: "If My people... humble themselves and pray"). Manasseh's repentance (2 Chr 33:10-13), which the Prayer of Manasseh expands.

## 1 Ezra (1 Esdras / Greek Esdras)
**Overview:** A Greek retelling from Josiah's Passover through the return from exile and Ezra's reforms. Its unique story is the contest of the three bodyguards of King Darius about what is strongest (wine, the king, women — and above all, truth) (3-4): "Great is truth, and stronger than all things" (4:41).
**Note:** In the bundled text it is "I Esdras". The book called "II Esdras" in the KJV Apocrypha (4 Ezra, an apocalypse) is *not* part of the OSB.

## 2 Ezra (Ezra) and Nehemiah
**Overview:** The return from Babylon under Zerubbabel, the rebuilding of the Temple (Ezra 1-6), Ezra's return and the call to keep the Law (7-10). Nehemiah rebuilds the walls of Jerusalem despite opposition (Neh 1-7), Ezra reads the Law to the people (8), confession and covenant renewal (9-10).
**Themes:** Restoration after repentance, rebuilding the house of God, the reading of Scripture in the assembly (Neh 8 as a model for the Liturgy of the Word).

## Tobit
**Overview:** A devout exile in Nineveh, blinded while burying the dead, sends his son Tobias on a journey. The Archangel Raphael, disguised, guides him, drives away a demon from Sarah, whom Tobias marries, and heals Tobit's eyes.
**Key passages:** Almsgiving "delivers from death" (4:10; 12:9). "What you hate, do not do to anyone" (4:15). Tobias and Sarah's prayer on their wedding night (8:4-8). Raphael: "I am one of the seven holy angels" (12:15).
**In the Church:** A model of marriage, care for the dead and the work of guardian angels. The Orthodox wedding service prays for the couple to be blessed "as You blessed Tobias and Sarah."

## Judith
**Overview:** When Holofernes' Assyrian army besieges the town of Bethulia, the widow Judith, after prayer and fasting, enters the enemy camp and beheads him, saving her people.
**Christ/Theotokos:** Hymns compare the Theotokos to Judith, through whom the enemy is crushed. A lesson in trusting God when everything seems lost (8:11-27).

## Esther (with the Greek additions)
**Overview:** The Jewish queen Esther saves her people from Haman's plot to destroy them. The Greek text adds Mordecai's dream, the royal letters, and the prayers of Mordecai and Esther, which name God explicitly (the Hebrew text never mentions God).
**Key passage:** "Who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (4:14). Esther's prayer (Addition C).
**Note:** In the bundled text, the Hebrew-based chapters are in "Esther" and the Greek parts are in "Additions to Esther" (KJV numbering 10:4-16:24).

## 1 Maccabees
**Overview:** Antiochus IV Epiphanes desecrates the Temple and forbids the Law (c. 167 BC). The priest Mattathias and his sons — Judas Maccabeus, Jonathan, Simon — lead the revolt, rededicate the Temple (origin of Hanukkah, 4:36-59) and win independence.
**Theme:** Faithfulness to God under persecution.

## 2 Maccabees
**Overview:** A separate account of the same events, with more theology: the martyrdom of the elderly Eleazar (6) and of the seven brothers and their mother (7), prayer and sacrifice for the dead (12:38-45), and the intercession of the departed (15:12-16).
**Key passages:** "God did not make them out of things that existed" (7:28; creation out of nothing). 12:43-45 supports the Orthodox practice of prayers and memorial services for the departed.
**Saints:** The Holy Maccabean Martyrs (Solomonia, her seven sons and Eleazar) are feasted on 1 August, beginning the Dormition Fast.

## 3 Maccabees
**Overview:** Not about the Maccabees: the Egyptian king Ptolemy IV tries to enter the Temple and later to kill the Jews of Alexandria with drunken elephants; God saves them in answer to prayer. Not included in the bundled text.

## 4 Maccabees (OSB appendix)
**Overview:** A philosophical sermon on "devout reason mastering the passions," using the martyrdom of Eleazar and the seven brothers. Important for early Christian ideas of martyrdom. Not in the bundled text.
