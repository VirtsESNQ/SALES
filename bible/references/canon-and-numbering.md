# Canon and numbering

## The Old Testament in the Orthodox Church

The Orthodox Old Testament follows the Septuagint (LXX), the Greek translation used by the Apostles and quoted most often in the New Testament. The OSB's Old Testament is a new translation from the LXX.

Orthodox Bibles group the books roughly like this (order as in the OSB):

- **Law:** Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy
- **History:** Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1-4 Kingdoms (= 1-2 Samuel, 1-2 Kings), 1-2 Chronicles (Paraleipomenon), 1 Ezra (the Greek Esdras), 2 Ezra (Ezra), Nehemiah, Tobit, Judith, Esther (with the Greek additions), 1-3 Maccabees
- **Wisdom/Poetry:** Psalms (151 psalms), Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Wisdom of Solomon, Wisdom of Sirach (Ecclesiasticus)
- **Prophets:** Hosea, Amos, Micah, Joel, Obadiah, Jonah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi (the Twelve, in LXX order), Isaiah, Jeremiah, Baruch, Lamentations, Epistle of Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel (with Susanna, Bel and the Dragon, and the Song of the Three Young Men)
- **Often printed in an appendix:** Prayer of Manasseh, 4 Maccabees

The Orthodox call the books beyond the Hebrew canon *anagignoskomena* ("books to be read"). They are read in church and fully part of the Bible, though some Fathers ranked them slightly differently. Avoid calling them "apocrypha" in an Orthodox context.

The naming of the Ezra/Esdras books varies between editions. When it matters, describe the content ("the book of Ezra-Nehemiah" or "the Greek Esdras that retells Josiah to Ezra") rather than relying on the number alone.

## The New Testament

The same 27 books as other Christians. The OSB uses the NKJV text, based on the Byzantine/majority Greek tradition.

## Numbering differences (LXX vs. Hebrew/MT)

### Psalms

| LXX (Orthodox) | Hebrew / most English Bibles |
|---|---|
| 1-8 | 1-8 |
| 9 | 9-10 |
| 10-112 | 11-113 |
| 113 | 114-115 |
| 114-115 | 116 |
| 116-145 | 117-146 |
| 146-147 | 147 |
| 148-150 | 148-150 |
| 151 | (none) |

Quick rule: for most Psalms between 10 and 147, the LXX number is one lower than the Hebrew number. So the Orthodox "Psalm 50" (the great penitential psalm) is Psalm 51 in most English Bibles, and Psalm 22 (LXX) "The Lord is my shepherd" is Psalm 23. Verse numbers can also shift by one or two because LXX often counts the superscription as verse 1.

### Other books

- **1-4 Kingdoms** = 1-2 Samuel and 1-2 Kings.
- **Jeremiah:** the LXX order differs; the oracles against the nations come after chapter 25 in the LXX, so chapter numbers from 25 to 51 don't line up with English Bibles. Check carefully.
- **Daniel:** Song of the Three Young Men is inserted in chapter 3 (so chapter 3 verse numbers run longer), Susanna and Bel are often chapters 13 and 14.
- **Esther:** the Greek additions are sometimes given letters (A-F) or folded into the chapters.
- **Malachi:** English Bibles have 4 chapters; Hebrew and many LXX editions have 3 (English 4:1-6 = 3:19-24).
- **Joel:** English 2:28-32 = Hebrew/LXX 3:1-5.

When in doubt, give both references: "Psalm 50 (51)".
